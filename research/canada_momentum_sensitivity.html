Canada MomentumParameter sensitivity analysis

One-at-a-time sensitivity sweeps across momentum lookback, market cap filter, portfolio size, regime window and fundamental filter, behind the Canada TSX momentum strategy. TSX (.TO), EODHD daily data, 2000–2026.

I. Universe & Method

Universe
Toronto Stock Exchange (.TO), EODHD daily adjusted prices, 1,766 tickers since 2000
Signal
N-month total return, no skip-month unless noted
Selection
Top K by signal, descending, after all eligibility filters
Rebalance
Monthly, end-of-month signal, executed at the next real trading day's close
Regime filter
TSX Composite (^GSPTSE) close vs its own N-day moving average
Regime rule
Above MA → fully invested / Below → 100% cash
Costs
40bps round-trip on monthly turnover (real IBKR median spread)
Period
2000–01 to 2026–07 (319 months)

The engine is audited for lookahead bias: every signal uses only data available as of its decision date, fundamentals enter the universe on their filing date (never the period date), and each month's return is generated by the position decided using the prior month's information, priced at the next real trading day's close — not the signal day's own close, which isn't achievable live.

Baseline for every sweep below: N=10, 12-month momentum (no skip), mcap ≥ 20th percentile (top 80% by size), NetIncome(TTM)>0, TSX Composite vs its own 75-day moving average, equal weighted, CDRs and preferred shares excluded, since 2000 (319 months) — CAGR +36.1%, Sharpe 1.35, Sortino 3.43, MaxDD −24.5%, matching the live strategy report exactly. There is no volatility-exclusion filter in the current config (tested and rejected earlier as overfit-prone — see Section VII) and no alternative weighting scheme has been re-tested against equal weight under the current engine.

A clear step up at 12 months, not a gradual drift: Sharpe climbs steadily from 3M (0.86) through 9M (1.03–1.07), then jumps to 1.35 at 12M — the biggest single move in this table. We tested the standard academic 12–1 convention (skip the most recent month, to avoid short-term reversal) against pure 12–0: no-skip wins clearly here, and not narrowly — better Sharpe (1.35 vs 1.13), better CAGR (+36.1% vs +31.0%), and a much shallower drawdown (−24.5% vs −37.6%). Skipping the most recent month costs more here than in most academic momentum studies; we kept pure 12–0.

Sharpe is essentially flat (1.35) across the three loosest floors tested (top 90/85/80%) and only starts eroding once the floor tightens past P20 — every step from top 75% down to top 60% is worse on every metric, monotonically. Inside that flat top-90–80% band the deciding factor is drawdown, not Sharpe: top 80% has the shallowest MaxDD of the three (−24.5% vs −26.2% and −29.1%) for the same risk-adjusted return, which is why it's kept over the even looser alternatives. We use a percentile filter rather than a fixed dollar floor specifically because a fixed floor gets stricter every year as the market grows and would need manual re-calibration; a percentile filter doesn't.

MA75 dominates on every metric tested — best Sharpe, best Sortino, best CAGR and best MaxDD simultaneously, which is not the usual shape of a regime-window sweep. The pattern either side of it is not monotonic: MA50 and MA200 both give the two worst drawdowns in the table (−44% and −46%), while MA150 sits in between with a MaxDD close to MA75's despite lower Sharpe — the window that reacts fastest and the window that reacts slowest both under-protect here, and the ones in between mostly trade Sharpe for no drawdown benefit. This matches what we found the last time this window was swept, before this session's engine fixes (calendar contamination, execution lag, return-date labeling) — MA75's edge over MA200 held up under both the old and the corrected engine, which is some evidence it isn't an artifact of any one bug.

N=10 is not the strict winner here, and we're not going to pretend it is. N=15 edges it out on Sharpe (1.36 vs 1.35) and has a meaningfully shallower MaxDD (−20.7% vs −24.5%), at the cost of about 3.3 points of CAGR. N=10 was kept for the extra return and the higher Sortino, on the view that this strategy is explicitly sized for concentrated conviction, not diversification — but N=15 is a legitimate, honestly-better-on-two-metrics alternative for an investor who weights drawdown more heavily than we do. It is not a case of one config dominating the other on every axis.

NetIncome>0 dominates every alternative tested, on every metric, including a new one added this round: ROE>0 (net income divided by trailing book equity), which came in a clear third behind NetIncome and EBIT. GrossProfit>0 barely screens anything meaningful out on the TSX — it's close to the unfiltered baseline on Sharpe (0.86 vs 0.82) and actually the worst MaxDD in the table (−37.7%), worse than having no fundamental filter at all. The ranking — NetIncome > EBIT > ROE > GrossProfit ≈ None — makes economic sense for this universe: TSX's small/mid-cap resource names routinely post positive gross profit and even positive EBIT on paper while burning cash below the operating line (interest, taxes, impairments), so the bottom-line filter is doing real work the top-line ones don't.

VII. A Filter We Tested and Dropped

Volatility-exclusion filter
An earlier version of this strategy excluded the most volatile slice of the eligible universe (252-day realized volatility) before ranking by momentum. It was dropped: sensitivity to the exclusion threshold was jagged and non-monotonic in a way more consistent with overfitting than with a real, generalizable effect, and it interacted with the market cap filter in ways that made the two hard to reason about independently. Simpler config, one fewer knob that could be tuned to a backtest.

VIII. Data Quality: What We Checked

CDR & preferred-share contamination
Canadian Depositary Receipts (foreign mega-caps like AAPL, NVDA, TSLA cross-listed on the TSX with huge global market cap but a tiny local float) and preferred-share series with fundamentals data that appears copied from their common-stock parent are both excluded by ticker pattern before ranking. Neither is common enough at N=10 to move the headline numbers meaningfully, but both are excluded on principle.
Extreme-return scan (this round)
A full scan of the 2000–2026 universe found 404 monthly ticker-returns beyond ±300%/−85% — almost all in names that were never mcap/NetIncome-eligible. Of the 319 months × 10 holdings actually held in the real backtest (4,375 ticker-months), exactly 4 coincided with an implausible price move, and all 4 fell in months the regime filter had already zeroed out (TSX below its 75-day MA) — none reached the published return series. Spot-checked online: one (Epsilon Energy, 2019) was a real, confirmed TSX delisting; one (GLG Life Tech, Dec 2007) was a real IPO-week price spike; one (an early-2000s ticker now reused by MTY Food Group) traces to a genuine shell-company reverse merger under a since-recycled symbol. Not all "extreme" events in this data are errors — but none of the ones that mattered to this backtest needed to be.

X. Limitations

  1. Portfolio size is a genuine trade-off, not a settled question (Section V): N=15 beats N=10 on Sharpe and MaxDD; N=10 was kept for CAGR and Sortino. A reasonable investor weighting drawdown more heavily could prefer N=15.
  2. Weighting scheme not re-tested this round. An earlier (pre-engine-fix) comparison of equal weight vs. inverse-volatility at a different N found a small Sharpe/MaxDD edge for inverse-vol and a CAGR edge for equal weight — not independently re-verified under the current engine or at N=10.
  3. Single-market backtest. Every number here is TSX-only and doesn't necessarily generalize to other markets we run (USA, UK) — each uses its own independently-tuned config.
  4. Sector concentration. The TSX is heavily weighted toward energy and materials; the current top-10 book is seven precious-metals names out of ten, reflecting where 12-month momentum happens to be right now, not a deliberate sector call. This is a real, live-tradeable exposure, not a footnote.
  5. Drawdown magnitude. −24.5% is a real number in this backtest, not a tail-risk footnote — this is a high-volatility, high-conviction strategy, not a low-vol product.
  6. No out-of-sample holdout. Every sweep in this article, and the final config it points to, was chosen by looking at the full 2000–2026 history. That's disclosed, not hidden: nothing here has been validated against data the selection process didn't see.
