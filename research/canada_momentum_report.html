XV. Risks & Caveats

  • Transaction costs included. 40bps round-trip on actual monthly turnover — the real IBKR-measured median spread across this strategy's historical holdings (see Section XVI), not a flat guess. Canada carries no stamp-duty-equivalent tax (unlike the UK strategy's SDRT), so this is spread cost only.
  • Parameter selection. MA75, N=15, 12M (no skip) momentum, the mcap percentile floor and the volatility-exclusion filter were informed by in-sample sensitivity analysis on the same dataset. The companion Parameter robustness → report documents how performance behaves across a range of reasonable values for each parameter, not just the final choice.
  • Benchmark is TSX Composite (^GSPTSE), price return only (no dividends). Strategy uses adjusted prices including dividends. Table columns above are labeled accordingly (TSX Composite Price Return / Relative Performance vs TSX Price Return) rather than "Alpha," since the strategy and benchmark are not on a like-for-like total-return basis.
  • Backtested performance is not a forecast. Live results may differ from the backtest due to parameter uncertainty, implementation costs, liquidity constraints, and model degradation over time.
  • Resource concentration. TSX is heavily weighted in energy and materials. Top 15 momentum stocks may be sector-concentrated in commodity cycles.
  • Data source: EODHD adjusted prices & market cap (shares × price). Adjusted for splits, dividends, and corporate actions.

XVI. Real Spread & Liquidity Robustness

Real spread measurement. On 2026-08-19, real bid/ask spreads were pulled via IBKR (delayed data) for the full historical universe of names this strategy has ever selected since 2000 (555 unique tickers). 369 returned a valid live quote (67% coverage) — the rest have since been delisted, renamed, or acquired and have no resolvable contract today (a number of TSX class-share tickers, e.g. HPS-A/BBD-B/TECK-B, initially failed due to a dash-vs-dot symbol formatting issue and were recovered on retry). Distribution across the 369 covered names: median 39.9bps, mean 136.0bps (a long right tail from genuinely illiquid small-caps, not outlier noise), IQR [16.8, 102.2]bps. This is a single point-in-time snapshot used as a static proxy for each ticker's liquidity at every historical month it was held — an approximation, not true point-in-time historical spread data.

A real-spread liquidity filter was tested and rejected. Unlike the UK strategy (where filtering to liquid-only names improved risk-adjusted returns), applying the same static-spread eligibility screen to Canada — excluding candidates above 30/50/70/100bps before ranking, N=15 — hurt performance at every threshold: gross CAGR fell from 34.0% (no filter) to 22.6-26.9% depending on cutoff, with Sharpe dropping from 1.40 to 1.08-1.24. A separate test raising the market-cap percentile floor (using real point-in-time historical market cap, not the spread proxy) showed the same pattern. Canada's percentile-based mcap universe (top 70% by size, not an absolute floor) deliberately reaches into smaller, less-liquid names, and that inclusion is where a meaningful part of the strategy's edge lives — restricting to liquid-only names shrinks the opportunity set more than it saves in execution cost. No liquidity filter was adopted; the live & backtested configuration (N=15, mcap percentile P30, no additional liquidity screen) is unchanged.
