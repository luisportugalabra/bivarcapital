XV. Risks & Caveats

  • Transaction costs. 40bps round-trip on actual monthly turnover, based on real IBKR median bid/ask spreads measured across this strategy's historical holdings. Costs on regime transitions (moving fully in or out of the market) are approximated from the same turnover-based model rather than measured separately, which likely understates the true cost of those transitions by a small margin.
  • Parameter selection. MA75, N=10, market-cap floor (top80%), and 12M (no skip) momentum were chosen via sensitivity analysis on the same dataset used to report performance here — no out-of-sample holdout. The companion parameter-robustness work documents how performance behaves across a range of reasonable values for each choice, not just the final one.
  • Benchmark is TSX Composite (^GSPTSE), price return only (no dividends). Strategy uses adjusted prices including dividends. Table columns above are labeled accordingly (TSX Composite Price Return / Relative Performance vs TSX Price Return) rather than "Alpha," since the strategy and benchmark are not on a like-for-like total-return basis.
  • Backtested performance is not a forecast. Live results may differ from the backtest due to parameter uncertainty, implementation costs, liquidity constraints, and model degradation over time.
  • Resource concentration. TSX is heavily weighted in energy and materials. Top 10 momentum stocks may be sector-concentrated in commodity cycles. Performance is not driven by a handful of outlier months: the ten best months (out of 319) are spread across 2003–2023, not concentrated in a single regime.
  • Data source: EODHD adjusted prices, market cap (shares × price), and quarterly financials (NetIncome, filing-date-gated to avoid look-ahead). Adjusted for splits, dividends, and corporate actions.

XVI. Fundamental Filter

NetIncome(TTM) > 0 is applied as a profitability screen alongside the market-cap floor. A sweep of five profitability filters at this MA75/mcap-top80% base (NetIncome, EBIT, GrossProfit, FreeCashFlow, ROE, all TTM, point-in-time via filing date) found NetIncome>0 gives the best combination of CAGR, Sharpe, and MaxDD among the five. GrossProfit>0 alone was notably weaker, letting through margin-positive-but-unprofitable names. A volatility- exclusion filter was also tested and set aside: it produced an unstable, non-monotonic effect on Sharpe and MaxDD across nearby exclusion thresholds, more consistent with fitting noise in this sample than a real, generalizable effect.
