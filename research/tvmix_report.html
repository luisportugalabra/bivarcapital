TV-Mix — US Trending ValueA value composite crossed with 12-month momentum, run in both directions

A long-only US equity strategy built from two signals — a five-metric value composite and 12-month momentum — applied in opposite order to form two legs of five names each. 10 equal-weighted positions, monthly rebalance, S&P 500 MA200 regime overlay, 20bps round-trip costs. 255 months (2005–2026) on Sharadar point-in-time fundamentals with delisted companies included.

I. The two legs

TV-C (classic) — value filters, momentum ranks.
Of the cheapest 5% of the eligible universe by composite, buy the 5 with the strongest 12-month momentum.

TV-M (inverse) — momentum filters, value ranks.
Of the 30% with the strongest 12-month momentum, buy the 5 cheapest by composite.

TV-Mix — both legs held together, 10 equal-weighted positions. A name selected by both legs carries double weight (typically 1–3 names of 10). The Mix is the published strategy; the legs are shown separately below because their parameter sensitivities differ.

Value composite

Five metrics, each converted to a cross-sectional percentile within the eligible universe that month, then averaged: P/E, P/B, P/S, EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF. A non-positive denominator (loss-making, negative equity) receives the worst possible percentile rather than being dropped — without this, loss-makers would rank as "cheapest". Missing metrics count as neutral (0.5); at least 3 valid metrics are required.

Shareholder yield was tested and removed. Dividend yield alone degraded the composite (15.3% → 13.2% CAGR); buyback yield alone also degraded it (→ 14.1%); only the two summed into one metric improved it, and that improvement disappeared when they entered as two separate metrics. A result that does not survive decomposition reads as noise. Removing yield also turned the value-percentile sensitivity from a spike into a plateau at the 10-name-per-leg configuration.

Universe and execution

  • US primary common stocks; market cap ≥ $500M; 20-day average dollar volume ≥ $2M; price > $1
  • Signal computed at the month-end close; position live from the following close (verified by tracing the index chain)
  • Regime: S&P 500 vs its 200-day moving average → 100% cash below. Invested 73% of months; 2000–02 and 2008 entirely in cash
  • Costs: 20bps round-trip on realised turnover (~330%/year). Median position: ~$1.6B market cap, ~$16M ADV

III. Reading the sensitivity honestly

What is genuinely flat. Momentum percentile is flat from 10% to 50% (Sharpe 0.78–0.85). Costs degrade smoothly and only break the strategy at 200bps, ten times the realistic level for $1–5B names. ADV threshold is flat up to $5M. The 12-month lookback beats 6 months in all three variants.
Five names per leg sits on a narrower ridge than ten. At 10 names per leg the value-percentile sweep was flat from 3% to 30%. At five names it holds from 3% to 10% and then collapses — the Mix falls to 10.0% CAGR at a 20% cut, and the classic leg to 5.5%. The reason is mechanical: widen the value bucket to ~470 names and the five highest-momentum names in it are momentum extremes, which in this universe means speculative junk. The tight value filter is what keeps the momentum rank honest. This is an argument for 10 names per leg if parameter safety matters more than concentration.
The regime MA is the fragile parameter. A plateau exists at 150–250 days, but MA100 loses 2.7pp of CAGR to whipsaw and MA300 loses 1.7pp with a far worse path. MA250 is the best in-sample cell (19.6% / 0.92 for the Mix) but part of that edge comes from where the average happened to cut in 2020. MA200, the middle of the plateau, is the published choice, and the honest expectation.
Market cap is not flat. $500M is the best cell; $2B loses 2.9pp. The edge lives in mid caps, and the floor sits at the top of that slope rather than in the centre of a plateau.

IV. How the backtest is built

  • Period: January 2005 to April 2026, 255 months, 316 rebalance dates — all verified to be real trading days, no weekends, 28–33 days apart.
  • Fundamentals: Sharadar SF1 TTM, indexed by availability rather than period end. Audited lag from filing date: median 61 days, zero negatives.
  • Survivorship: the universe holds 54% dead tickers; 40% of names ever selected no longer exist (Marathon Oil, Tech Data, US Steel, Sears), carried at their real returns with no forward-filling after delisting.
  • Data quality: one single daily move above 50% across 21 years of holdings — no sign of the split or sentinel corruption found in other vendors.
  • Weights: verified to sum to exactly 1.0 with no negative positions.

V. Risks and limitations

No sealed holdout. The strategy was designed and tested repeatedly over the full 2005–2026 sample within a single research session. Every figure carries selection bias from that process. Forward performance should be expected below the backtest, not at it.
  • The live signal uses a different data source. The backtest runs on Sharadar; the live signal runs on the TradingView screener so it can execute without local data. P/B is fiscal-quarter rather than TTM, momentum comes from TradingView's 12-month performance field, and the regime uses the S&P 500 price index rather than SPY total return. Live picks will closely resemble the backtest but will not reproduce it exactly.
  • Concentration. Ten positions, of which 1–3 carry double weight. Removing the five best contributors of all time takes CAGR from 17.3% to 15.2% in the 20-name version; the 10-name version is more exposed still.
  • Turnover is high (~330%/year, median holding around two months). This is affordable only because the names are liquid.
  • Returns are concentrated in recoveries. The strategy earns most of its excess in the years following market panics and lags in mega-cap melt-ups (2019: −26pp vs SPY; 2024: −16pp).
  • It is currently cold. 2025 was the weakest year of the series, following a weak 2024.
  • The regime filter carries part of the Sharpe. The strategy was fully in cash through 2000–02 and 2008; its behaviour while invested is measured over fewer years than the headline period suggests.
