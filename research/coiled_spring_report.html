The Idea

The "coiled spring" metaphor comes from physics: compress a spring under tension long enough and it releases with force. In markets, the analogue is a stock where the fundamentals have been quietly improving for a year — expanding margins, rising returns on capital, accelerating gross profit growth — while the stock price has gone nowhere for six months. The price compression is the coil. The improving business is the tension building inside it.

When that tension releases — when the market finally notices — the move tends to be sharp and sustained. The strategy's job is to identify these stocks before the release, hold through the move, and exit when fundamentals begin to deteriorate.

The core insight: fundamental improvement precedes price movement. Most investors wait for the price to confirm the improvement. This strategy buys the gap between the two.

Three layers of logic

1 — Quality Signal
Rank stocks by composite fundamental improvement. Top ranks enter the eligible pool.
2 — Price Filter
Only consider stocks where 6-month price return is between -15% and +15%. Price must be coiled, not already running.
3 — Regime Filter
S&P 500 must be above its 225-day MA. In bear markets, all positions close and capital moves to GLD.

Signal Construction

After testing 86 combinations of fundamental metrics (see Signal Sweep below), the optimal composite — v2 Signal — combines three components equally weighted:

# All computed with 12-month lag (TTM vs TTM one year ago)
# Winsorized cross-sectionally (p1–p99) before ranking

nm_growth = rank( net_margin_ttm / net_margin_ttm.shift(12) − 1 )
            # Net margin growth YoY: is the business becoming more profitable?

roic_lvl  = rank( ebit / (equity + debt) )
            # ROIC absolute level: is capital employed efficiently?
            # Uses level (not trend) — high ROIC now matters more than direction

gm_accel  = rank( (gm − gm.shift(12)) − (gm.shift(12) − gm.shift(24)) )
            # Gross margin 2nd derivative: is the improvement accelerating?
            # Captures the "getting better faster" dynamic in unit economics

signal    = (nm_growth + roic_lvl + gm_accel) / 3

Design decisions

Why ROIC level, not trend?
A company with 25% ROIC flat beats one with 5% ROIC improving. We want capital compounders. The trend is captured by net margin growth.
Why gross margin 2nd derivative?
First derivative = direction. Second derivative = acceleration. We want businesses where the unit economics are not just good but getting better faster. This leads the net income signal by 2–4 quarters.
TTM vs quarterly?
All metrics use trailing-twelve-months (TTM) figures. This smooths seasonality and makes cross-sector comparisons meaningful. Gross margin acceleration uses TTM-to-TTM deltas.
Look-ahead bias prevention
Each fundamental observation is mapped to its avail_ym field (the month the data became publicly available) with forward-fill. Earnings releases typically lag quarter-end by 30–60 days. This is accounted for.

Timing mechanics

Each month, the loop processes in strict order to avoid look-ahead bias:

for month M in test_months:
    # 1. Record return: use whatever was held at START of M
    ret[M] = mean_return(held_positions) or GLD_return if empty

    # 2. Check regime at END of M
    if SP500[M] < MA225[M]:
        close_all_positions()   # M+1 goes to GLD
        continue

    # 3. Check exits for each held position
    for each position:
        if signal_rank < 0.40 or cumulative_return <= -0.25:
            exit position

    # 4. Fill empty slots with new entries
    eligible = (MCap ≥ 1B) AND (netinc > 0) AND (-15% ≤ ret6m ≤ +15%)
    eligible.exclude(already_held)
    new_entries = top_signal[eligible].take(20 - len(held))

Key finding: Adding valuation signals (PE, PB, EV/EBITDA) to the v2 composite reduces Sharpe to 0.61–0.75. The strategy already captures quality-at-a-price through the price compression filter — overlaying explicit valuation creates conflict and removes good entries.

Bear Market Behaviour

The regime filter (S&P 500 vs MA225) switches the portfolio to GLD when the broad market turns bear. This is the primary source of drawdown protection.

The 2011 loss is the strategy's most instructive failure: the S&P 500 declined but never broke MA225 long enough to trigger the regime filter. The portfolio remained fully invested in small/mid growth names which sold off sharply. The bear filter catches sustained bear markets but not sharp corrections that recover quickly.

What a Position Looks Like

A typical entry: a $3B industrial company where gross margins expanded from 28% to 32% over 12 months (unit economics improving), ROIC is 18% (absolute top quartile), and net margin grew 40% YoY. The stock is flat over six months — the market hasn't noticed yet. The signal ranks it in the top 5% of the universe. The strategy buys at end-of-month and holds until:

  • The signal rank drops below the 40th percentile (business quality deteriorated vs peers), or
  • The position is down 25% from entry price, or
  • The S&P 500 closes a month below its 225-day MA.

The strategy does not take profit. There is no target price. Positions are held as long as the fundamental thesis remains intact and the market regime is bull.
