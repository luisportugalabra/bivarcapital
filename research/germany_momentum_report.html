Germany MomentumXETRA strategy report

A momentum strategy on Deutsche Börse XETRA: buy the ten German-domiciled companies with the strongest twelve-month return, equal weighted, rebalance monthly, stand aside when the DAX is below its 200-day average. 2000–2026, 319 months. Live since August 2026. It is presently 28.1% below its May 2026 high — the deepest drawdown in the record — and down 18.9% on the year. That is where the report starts, because it is the most useful thing to know about it today.

I. Where it stands

The strategy was up 13.4% through May. June cost 13.4% and July another 17.0%, wiping the year and more. August and September have been flat. YTD -18.9%.

Two months took back fourteen months of gains. This is not a malfunction — it is the shape of the return distribution the strategy has always had, and Section V sets out how often it happens. What is unusual is the depth: 28.1% is the largest peak-to-trough fall in 27 years of history, and it is not over.

II. The rule

Everything, with nothing omitted:

  1. Universe. XETRA, German-domiciled companies only. Domicile is read from the ISIN country prefix, not the exchange's own country field — that field tags every US company cross-listed in Frankfurt as German.
  2. Size. Keep the top 70% by market capitalisation, measured against that month's own universe rather than a fixed euro floor.
  3. Signal. Twelve-month total return, no skip month. Rank, take the top ten.
  4. Weights. Equal, 10% each. No volatility targeting, no conviction sizing.
  5. Timing. Rank on the last trading day of the month; buy at the close of the next.
  6. Regime. If the DAX closed below its 200-day moving average on the signal day, hold cash for the month instead.
  7. Liquidity. Not filtered. A 60-day average euro turnover is computed and printed beside each name so the constraint is visible when the order is placed.

No fundamental screen, no stop loss, no target. The regime filter is the only thing that ever takes the book to cash, and it has done so in 1% of months.

IV. Why it returns what it returns

The number that explains the strategy is not the CAGR, it is the asymmetry:
Upside capture (months the DAX rose)0.80
Downside capture (months the DAX fell)0.14
Beta0.35

It takes 80% of the index's up months and 14% of its down months. Almost all of that comes from the regime filter: in a falling market it is usually in cash, so the down months it does participate in are the ones where the DAX fell while its own trend filter was still on.

That asymmetry is the whole edge, and it is also the fragility. A market that falls fast enough to hurt before the 200-day average turns — June and July of this year — is the case the filter cannot catch, because a 200-day average by construction lags a two-month move.

At €50,000 a position the worst becomes 14.6%, which is a day or two of patient working rather than a single order.

The practical ceiling is not the ten names held but the tail of the ranking they are drawn from. Costs are modelled at 40bps round trip against a measured median spread of 35bps across the eligible universe. Sub-€2M of capital is comfortable; beyond that the thin names start to bind.

IX. What would tell me it has stopped working

  1. A rolling three-year return below zero. Over the whole history that has happened in 8% of windows, with a worst of -10%. Two consecutive negative three-year windows would be outside anything in the record.
  2. The regime filter failing to protect. The case for this strategy over buying the DAX rests on 0.14 downside capture. If a bear market arrives and the book participates fully, the thesis is gone regardless of what the CAGR says.
  3. Drawdown past 45%. The record's worst is -39.1% and the current episode is at 28%. Beyond 45% the strategy would be outside its own history and I would not assume mean reversion.
  4. Turnover rising without return. Roughly three of ten names change every month; if that climbs while the excess over the DAX does not, the signal is chasing noise.

X. Limitations

  1. Pre-2015 is survivors only. The XETRA dataset records no delisting before 2015 — the entire Neuer Markt collapse is absent. Coverage is externally verified complete from 2018. The 2015–2026 window returns +18.3%, close to the headline, which is reassuring but not proof.
  2. N=10 is the practical choice, not the optimal one. N=20 returns the same CAGR at Sharpe 0.90 and a -27.8% drawdown. Ten is run because twenty positions is a lot of monthly turnover and the extra ten are largely unfillable. The eleven points of drawdown is the price.
  3. The configuration was selected in-sample across the same 2000–2026 history. The plateaus in the sensitivity analysis are the mitigation — the parameters sit on broad shelves rather than spikes — not a cure.
  4. Drawdowns are measured at month ends. The intra-month low is worse.
  5. Two months of live history. Everything before August 2026 is simulation.
