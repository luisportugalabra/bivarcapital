Strategy Construction

Every design choice is anchored to real-world constraints specific to LSE equity trading: stamp duty, mid-cap bid-ask spreads, UK IFRS filing timelines, and currency denomination. The strategy is intended to be executable by a retail or institutional investor with a brokerage account providing access to London-listed shares.

Signal generation — step by step

  1. Universe definition: All LSE-listed common stocks denominated in GBX (pence sterling). Excluded: ADRs, ETFs, investment trusts, REITs structured as closed-end funds, and any share class denominated in a foreign currency (USD, EUR, etc.).
  2. Market cap filter: Retain stocks where price × shares outstanding > £250M at each rebalance date. This is computed dynamically from current data — not a static list — so companies that grow into or fall out of the threshold are handled correctly over time.
  3. Profitability filter: Require EBIT (trailing twelve months) > 0. Financials are sourced with a 45-day filing lag to eliminate look-ahead bias: only results published at least 45 calendar days before the rebalance date are admitted.
  4. Momentum score calculation: Each qualifying stock receives a composite score: Score = 0.5 × R(6M) + 0.5 × R(12M), where R(6M) and R(12M) are total price returns (adjusted for dividends and corporate actions) over the prior 6 and 12 months respectively, skipping the most recent month to avoid short-term reversal contamination.
  5. Regime evaluation: Check whether the FTSE 100 index close on the rebalance date is above or below its 200-day simple moving average. This determines the portfolio allocation mode.
  6. Portfolio selection — bullish regime: Rank all filtered stocks by composite momentum score, descending. Select the top N = 12 stocks. Allocate equal weight (~8.33% each).
  7. Portfolio selection — bearish regime: Liquidate all equity positions. Allocate 100% to GLD (SPDR Gold Shares ETF, NYSE Arca ticker: GLD).
  8. Rebalance execution: Execute on the last trading day of each calendar month. Full portfolio rebalance: sell all positions exiting the top-12, buy all entering positions. Order execution is modelled at closing prices with trading costs applied per transaction.

Look-Ahead Bias Prevention

Avoiding look-ahead bias is the most critical discipline in any backtest that incorporates fundamental data. The UK strategy explicitly handles two distinct sources of potential contamination.

1. Accounting data — 45-day filing lag

UK-listed companies under IFRS are required to publish half-year results within three months and full-year results within four months of the period end. In practice, most large and mid-cap companies report within 45–60 days. The strategy adopts a conservative 45-day filing lag: on any rebalance date, only financial results whose period end falls at least 45 calendar days prior are used. Results published more recently are treated as unavailable.

This means, for example, that if a company's quarter ended 30 June, its EBIT figure is not admitted to the filter until at least 14 August, regardless of when it was actually published. This prevents the backtest from using numbers the investor could not have known at the time of decision.

2. Price data — point-in-time returns

Momentum returns are computed exclusively from adjusted closing prices available before the rebalance execution date. The return window deliberately skips the most recent month: the 6-month return is measured from month t−7 to t−1, and the 12-month return from t−13 to t−1. This design choice eliminates short-term reversal bias (the well-documented tendency for last month's winners to underperform in the immediate next period) and ensures no current-month price information contaminates the signal.

No survivorship bias: The backtest universe includes stocks that were later delisted, suspended, acquired, or went into administration during the covered period. Excluding these would systematically inflate historical returns by removing the worst outcomes. Every such stock remains in the eligible universe until the date it ceased trading.

Regime Filter & Gold Allocation

Cross-sectional momentum strategies carry elevated tail risk during broad market sell-offs. When the market falls sharply, momentum longs (recent winners) often experience sharp drawdowns as investors de-lever and rotate into defensives. The regime filter is designed to sidestep these episodes entirely.

Mechanics

On each month-end rebalance date, the FTSE 100 index closing level is compared to its 200-day simple moving average:

  • Bullish (FTSE 100 > MA200): Allocate to the top-12 momentum equity portfolio as described above.
  • Bearish (FTSE 100 ≤ MA200): Liquidate all equity positions. Rotate 100% into GLD (SPDR Gold Shares ETF).

Transitions between regimes are executed at the closing price on the rebalance date itself. No partial allocations or gradual transitions are applied — the switch is binary.

Why gold rather than cash

Holding cash during defensive periods eliminates market risk but forgoes return. Gold, by contrast, has historically delivered positive or near-flat real returns during equity bear markets and exhibits negative tail correlation with equities during risk-off episodes. Allocating to GLD converts the defensive period from dead capital into an active return source — while preserving the regime filter's primary goal of avoiding momentum equity drawdowns.

The GLD ETF is used (rather than physical gold or futures) for its simplicity, liquidity, and compatibility with a standard brokerage account. Note that GLD is USD-denominated, introducing GBP/USD currency exposure during defensive periods. In practice, USD has tended to strengthen during global risk-off events, providing an additional tailwind to the GLD allocation.

MA200 selection — grid search result

The grid search tested MA50, MA100, MA150, MA200, and MA250 as candidate regime thresholds. MA200 delivered the highest Sharpe ratio across all 125 tested sub-combinations. Shorter MAs (50, 100) generate excessive whipsawing — triggering bearish signals during normal pullbacks and missing the subsequent recovery. MA250 reacts too slowly to genuine bear markets, remaining invested too long before triggering.

Grid Search Methodology

All strategy parameters were selected through an exhaustive grid search over the full backtest period. No parameter was chosen by convention or intuition — every design decision was empirically validated across hundreds of combinations.

Search space

625 total combinations: 5 momentum configurations × 5 market cap thresholds × 5 portfolio sizes (N) × 5 MA regime thresholds

What was optimised

The primary optimisation target was the Sharpe ratio over the full Dec 2004 – Jul 2026 period, with secondary consideration given to CAGR and maximum drawdown. Combinations were ranked by Sharpe to identify the global optimum and to understand the sensitivity of performance to each parameter independently.

Momentum configuration

The 50/50 composite of 6M and 12M return outperformed pure 6M-only, pure 12M-only, and asymmetric composites (25/75, 75/25) on Sharpe ratio. The composite captures both medium-term trend continuation and longer-term fundamental momentum, while dampening the noisier signals that arise from relying on a single lookback window.

Market cap threshold

The £250M threshold delivered the best Sharpe ratio among all tested alternatives (£100M, £500M, £1B, £2B). It strikes the optimal balance: large enough to ensure adequate liquidity and reduce micro-cap noise, small enough to preserve access to high-momentum mid-cap names where price discovery is slower. The £250M floor supports portfolios up to ~£10M–20M AUM without meaningful market impact.

Regime filter threshold

MA200 was the top-ranked regime filter across all 125 sub-combinations. Shorter windows (MA50, MA100) produce too many false bearish signals during normal volatility; longer windows (MA250) are too slow to respond to genuine bear market conditions, leaving equity exposure in place for too long after the trend breaks.

Portfolio size (N)

The Sharpe curve peaks at N = 12 and declines on both sides. At smaller N (5–7), idiosyncratic stock risk dominates and drawdowns are severe. At larger N (20–25), the signal is diluted by lower-ranked momentum stocks, reducing both return and Sharpe. N=12 represents the optimal balance between signal concentration and diversification.

Trading Costs

All backtest performance figures are net of trading costs. Costs are applied at each individual trade, not as an annualised flat haircut. The cost model reflects the actual structure of LSE equity trading for a UK-based investor.

Cost structure per trade

Buy-side — 0.65% total

  • Stamp Duty Reserve Tax (SDRT): 0.50% — A mandatory UK government tax charged on all purchases of UK-incorporated shares settled via CREST. Applied at 0.5% of consideration. Non-reclaimable in any account type, including ISAs and SIPPs.
  • Bid-ask spread (buy side): 0.15% — Estimated half-spread for LSE mid-cap equities at time of execution. Represents the cost of taking liquidity by crossing the spread to fill a market order.

Sell-side — 0.15% total

  • Stamp duty on sells: 0% — SDRT applies exclusively to purchases. Sales are not subject to SDRT.
  • Bid-ask spread (sell side): 0.15% — Same half-spread estimate as the buy side, applied when liquidating a position. Represents the cost of selling at the bid rather than the mid-price.

Round-trip cost per position: ~0.80% (0.65% entry + 0.15% exit). With N=12 positions and approximately 30–40% monthly turnover, annual trading cost drag is roughly 2–3% per year. This drag is already reflected in the headline +23.7% CAGR figure — the gross pre-cost CAGR is higher.

Note on ISA and SIPP eligibility: Stamp duty applies even inside a Stocks & Shares ISA or SIPP. The 0.5% SDRT is charged at the CREST settlement layer and cannot be avoided through account type. Platform dealing commissions and foreign exchange fees (relevant when exiting to GLD) are not included in these estimates and will vary by broker. Investors should add their broker's per-trade commission on top of the modelled costs.

Additional Filter Research

Revenue growth filter

A secondary research pass evaluated adding a revenue growth filter (TTM revenue YoY > 0) as a supplementary screen on top of the primary EBIT profitability filter. Key findings:

  • Sharpe improvement: +0.006 above the base strategy — a marginal but consistent gain.
  • Marginally lower maximum drawdown (typically 1–2pp improvement), suggesting the filter removes some distressed companies that pass the EBIT screen.
  • Slight reduction in CAGR due to narrowing of the investable universe — fewer stocks qualify, reducing portfolio turnover opportunities.
  • Not included in the headline strategy: the +0.006 Sharpe improvement is within the noise margin for a 21.6-year backtest and could reflect sample-specific overfitting.

The revenue growth filter is documented here for transparency. It may be promoted to the live strategy if it demonstrates consistent improvement across rolling out-of-sample windows. As of the current specification, the strategy uses only the EBIT > 0 profitability filter.

Risk Considerations

Momentum crashes

Momentum strategies are historically vulnerable to sharp, sudden reversals — momentum crashes — that typically occur at the onset of strong market recoveries after deep bear markets. At these inflection points, recent losers (which momentum strategies are short on, implicitly) surge while recent winners lag. The regime filter mitigates but does not eliminate this risk: when the FTSE 100 re-enters bullish territory and the strategy rotates from GLD back to equities, the initial re-entry period can be turbulent if the recovery is rapid and concentrated in beaten-down sectors.

Concentration risk

At N=12, each position represents approximately 8.3% of the portfolio. A single adverse event in one holding (profit warning, regulatory action, fraud) can materially impact monthly performance. This concentration is the inherent cost of the high Sharpe ratio that momentum concentration produces — more diversified portfolios (N=20+) reduce this risk but sacrifice return.

Regime filter lag

The MA200 regime filter reacts with a lag. During a fast market crash, the strategy may remain in equities for several weeks before the FTSE 100 falls below its 200-day moving average and the defensive rotation triggers. The −33.0% maximum drawdown reflects this lag: the worst observed drawdown occurred during a rapid market decline before the regime filter could respond (most likely during the 2008–2009 financial crisis or early 2020).

Currency exposure in defensive mode

GLD is USD-denominated. When the strategy is in bearish regime and holding GLD, GBP/USD exchange rate movements affect sterling returns. A strengthening GBP reduces GLD returns in sterling terms; a weakening GBP amplifies them. Historically, global risk-off environments have tended to strengthen the USD, providing a partial additional tailwind to GLD holdings during equity bear markets. However, this cannot be guaranteed for future periods.

GLD operational considerations

GLD trades on NYSE Arca, requiring either a US-market-enabled brokerage account or access through a platform that offers GLD as a holdable security for UK investors. Some UK platforms offer a GLD equivalent through a London-listed gold ETC (e.g., SGLN or PHAU). Investors should verify that their platform supports the specific instrument before relying on the GLD signal.
