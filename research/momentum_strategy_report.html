I. Strategy Specification

Signal Day: 18th of each month (after market close)

  1. Regime check: Is the S&P 500 close above its 250-day simple moving average?
    • NO → Sell all stocks. Buy GLD (SPDR Gold Shares). Hold until next month’s signal day.
    • YES → Proceed to step 2.
  2. Compute momentum for every eligible stock:
    Momentum = 50% × (price / price 126 trading days ago − 1) + 50% × (price / price 252 trading days ago − 1)
  3. Filter universe:
    • Market capitalisation > $10 billion
    • EBIT > 0 (operating profit positive, most recent report)
    • US domestic common stock only
  4. Select top 7 by momentum composite. No sector cap.
  5. Execute on day 19: sell positions that left the portfolio, buy new ones. Equal weight (~14.3% each).
Why momentum works: Stocks that have risen tend to continue rising. Institutional capital flows are slow — funds take weeks to build positions. Information propagates gradually. Behavioural biases cause investors to sell winners too early and hold losers too long.
Why no sector cap: Tested max 2, 3, 4, 5 and no limit. No sector cap consistently performs equal or better across all periods. The sector cap forced diversification into weaker stocks that fell harder during crashes. Additionally, Sharadar (backtest) and TradingView (production) use different sector taxonomies, making sector caps behave inconsistently between backtest and live.
Why GLD during bear markets: Gold has near-zero correlation with the momentum strategy. During bear markets, gold averages +1.5%/month while stocks fall. The MA250 regime filter identifies bear markets early — switching to GLD preserves capital and generates positive returns during drawdowns.
Why signal day 18: Tested all trading days of the month (1st through last). Day 18 consistently produced the highest Sharpe ratio, likely because it captures momentum before end-of-month institutional rebalancing flows. Execution on day 19 ensures no look-ahead bias.

VIII. Why Each Parameter

Momentum blend: 50% × 6-month + 50% × 12-month

Combining two horizons captures both short-term acceleration and medium-term trend. Tested against pure 6m, 9m, 12m, and multiple weighted blends. This combination delivered the highest Sharpe ratio with daily data and realistic execution.

Market cap > $10B

Below $10B, momentum captures noise — pump-and-dumps, illiquid stocks, micro-caps with unreliable data. Above $10B, momentum reflects genuine institutional capital flows. The $8–12B range was tested; $10B is the sweet spot for Sharpe.

EBIT > 0

Filters out money-losing companies. Without this filter, momentum picks up hyped stocks with no fundamentals. Removing the EBIT filter reduces Sharpe by 0.10–0.15 in testing.

No sector cap

Tested max 2, 3, 4, 5 and no limit across multiple periods (1998–2026, 2005–2026). No sector cap consistently performs equal or better: similar CAGR and Sharpe, with better or equal drawdown. The sector cap forced diversification into weaker momentum stocks that actually fell harder during crashes. Removing it simplified the strategy without any cost.

MA250 regime filter

When the S&P 500 closes below its 250-day moving average, the strategy exits all stock positions. Without this filter, maximum drawdown exceeds −60%. With it, drawdown is contained to -29.1%.

GLD during bear months

Gold has near-zero correlation with the momentum strategy over the full sample. During the 55 bear months, GLD provided positive returns on average. Holding GLD instead of cash adds approximately +4% CAGR without materially increasing drawdown.

X. Risks & Caveats

  • Transaction costs not included. Approximately 84 stock trades per year plus ~4 GLD rotations. Estimated impact: −0.5% to −1.0% CAGR.
  • Overfitting risk. Multiple parameter combinations were tested. Expect 20–30% Sharpe degradation in live trading.
  • 21 years, 2 bear markets. Both were V-shaped recoveries (2008, 2020). A slow bear market (2000–2003 style) may behave differently.
  • 2024 dominates recent returns. The strategy returned ~+180% in 2024. Without this outlier year, CAGR is approximately +22%.
  • Concentration risk. 7 stocks at 14.3% each. A single-stock blowup costs ~14% of the portfolio. No sector cap means tech concentration is possible.
  • Realistic expectation post-costs: CAGR +22–26%, Sharpe 0.75–0.90.
