The Logic

Our valuation deciles research showed that of five metrics tested (P/E, P/S, P/B, EV/EBITDA, Shareholder Yield), only two have predictive power in the last 21 years:

Price-to-Sales (P/S)
Revenue is the hardest number to manipulate. Low P/S = more revenue per dollar of market cap. Spread: +7.4% (D1 vs D10, >$200M).
Shareholder Yield
Dividends + buybacks = total cash returned to shareholders. Companies returning the most cash outperform. Spread: +5.9% (>$5B).

We combine these two into a simple composite (average of percentile ranks), then add a 6-month momentum filter: from the cheapest 20% by PS+SY, buy the 25 stocks with the highest 6-month price momentum. This avoids value traps (cheap stocks still falling) and captures mean reversion (cheap stocks starting to recover).

Why This Works

1. P/S captures real value

Revenue is the cleanest fundamental. It doesn't depend on depreciation schedules, one-time charges, or accounting choices. A stock cheap on P/S genuinely generates more revenue per dollar of market cap.

2. Shareholder Yield captures capital return

Companies returning cash through dividends and buybacks are signalling confidence in their business. High shareholder yield is both a valuation signal (cheap enough to buy back stock) and a quality signal (generating enough cash to return it).

3. Momentum avoids value traps

The biggest risk in value investing is buying stocks that are cheap for a reason. Adding a 6-month momentum filter ensures we only buy cheap stocks that have started to recover — the market has begun to re-price them upward, confirming the value thesis.

4. Two factors is enough

More factors ≠ better signal. The VC2 uses six factors, four of which have zero or negative predictive power in the last 21 years. Including them dilutes the signal from the two that work. Simplicity wins.

The Strategy in Practice

Step 1 — Universe
All US equities >$5B market cap, excluding financials. ~350–750 stocks.
Step 2 — Value screen
Rank by P/S percentile + Shareholder Yield percentile (inverted). Average the two ranks. Take the cheapest 20% (~70–150 stocks).
Step 3 — Momentum filter
From the value pool, calculate 6-month price return (skipping the most recent month). Select the top 25 by momentum.
Step 4 — Hold
Equal weight. Hold for 1 month. Repeat.
