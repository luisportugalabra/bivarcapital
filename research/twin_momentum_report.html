Twin MomentumWe replicated the paper. It works — with caveats.

Huang, Zhang, Zhou & Zhu (2017) propose combining price momentum with "fundamental momentum" — trends in 7 accounting variables. They report 2.16% monthly spread. We replicated it out-of-sample on 2008–2026 data from Sharadar. The arithmetic returns match. The real-world compounded returns are lower.

Twin Momentum L/S: +21.8% arithmetic  /  +9.3% CAGR  ·  Paper: 2.16%/mo
25 stocks long-short · Monthly rebalance · Out-of-sample 2008–2026 · >$200M

What Is Twin Momentum?

The paper constructs a Fundamental Implied Return (FIR) for each stock using 7 accounting variables (ROE, ROA, earnings per share, accrual-based profitability, cash-based profitability, gross profitability, net payout yield) and their moving averages over 1, 2, 4, and 8 quarters — 28 predictors total.

A cross-sectional regression estimates which fundamental trends predict next month's return. The FIR score ranks stocks by their expected fundamental-driven return.

Twin momentum = stocks that rank high on BOTH price momentum (12-2) AND fundamental momentum (FIR). Buy the top, short the bottom.

The Arithmetic vs Geometric Gap

The twin momentum strategy has a standard deviation of 50.4% per year — three times the S&P 500's volatility. This creates an enormous gap between arithmetic mean (+21.8%) and geometric mean / CAGR (+9.3%).

The formula geometric ≈ arithmetic − variance/2 confirms: 21.8% − 25.4%/2 = 9.1% ≈ 9.3% actual CAGR. The math checks out.

The crash of 2009 (−79.9%) is the main culprit. Without it, the CAGR jumps to +20.1%. The Validea implementation (+2,702% since 2008, ~22% CAGR) likely avoids or mitigates this crash year somehow.

Key Findings

1. Fundamental momentum is real and replicable

Our out-of-sample fundamental momentum return (0.86%/mo arithmetic) almost exactly matches the paper's in-sample result (0.88%/mo). This is rare — most academic anomalies degrade significantly out of sample.

2. Price momentum is broken in the short leg

Pure price momentum L/S lost money in our period. The short leg (shorting past losers) gets destroyed during V-shaped recoveries (2009, 2020). The long leg works, but the shorts blow up.

3. Twin momentum has extreme volatility

With 25 stocks per side, annual std dev is 50%. This means years like +146% (2024) but also −80% (2009). The arithmetic return is impressive but the CAGR is halved by the volatility drag.

4. The arithmetic vs geometric gap is the real story

Academic papers report arithmetic means. Real investors earn geometric returns. A strategy with 50% volatility loses ~12% per year to this gap alone. Any implementation needs to reduce volatility (more stocks, position limits, risk management) to capture the arithmetic edge.
