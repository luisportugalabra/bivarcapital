# Chapter 15 — Value Composites (VC1, VC2, VC3)

## What the Book Claims (1964–2009)

### VC1 (P/B + P/E + P/S + EBITDA/EV + P/CF)
- D1 All Stocks: **17.18% CAGR**, $10,000 → $14,688,089
- Sharpe: **0.67** vs 0.33 universe
- "Better than any single value factor"
- Base rates: **98% rolling 5yr, 100% rolling 10yr**

### VC2 (VC1 + Shareholder Yield)
- D1 All Stocks: **17.30% CAGR**, $10,000 → $15,416,651
- Sharpe: **0.72**
- "Marginally better than VC1, lower volatility, better downside protection"

## What We Found (2005–2026, Quarterly TTM, 12-month averaged annual)

### Annual Rebalance (12 portfolios averaged)
| | >$200M | >$5B |
|---|---|---|
| **VC1** | +4.9% | +9.7% |
| **VC2** | +5.6% | +9.8% |
| **VC3** (+ div yield) | +5.2% | +9.3% |
| S&P 500 | ~8.4% | ~8.4% |

### Monthly Rebalance
| | >$200M CAGR | >$200M Sharpe | >$5B CAGR | >$5B Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VC1** | +10.8% | 0.50 | +10.2% | 0.56 |
| **VC2** | +12.1% | 0.56 | +10.0% | 0.56 |
| **VC3** | +8.9% | 0.46 | +11.8% | 0.64 |

## The Verdict

**The composites work — but not at the level the book promised.**

With annual rebalance (the book's methodology), VC1 and VC2 deliver ~+5% CAGR at >$200M — a 12% shortfall from the book's 17%. At >$5B they deliver ~10%, still 7% below the claim.

Monthly rebalance with quarterly TTM data significantly improves results — VC2 reaches +12.1% at >$200M. But this is a different strategy from what the book describes (annual rebalance).

The Sharpe ratio of 0.56 is respectable but not the 0.67–0.72 the book reported.

### Why the Composite Underperforms Its Components

Counter-intuitively, VC2 at >$5B (+10.0%) underperforms its best single component (Buyback Yield at +12.5%). This is because:

1. **P/E, P/B contribute zero or negative spread** — they dilute the composite
2. **EBITDA/EV is dead** (+0.6% spread at >$5B) — another diluter
3. **Only P/S and Buyback Yield carry the weight** — 2 out of 6 factors

A composite of 6 factors where 4 are broken will underperform a strategy using only the 2 that work. This is the central finding of our research.
