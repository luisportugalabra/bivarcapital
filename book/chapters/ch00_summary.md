## What Survived
1. **P/S** — the only single value factor with consistent predictive power
2. **Buyback Yield** — companies shrinking share count outperform
3. **Momentum** — strongest factor, especially at >$5B
4. **Trending Value** — combining value + momentum still works, but at ~14% not 21%

## What Died
1. **P/E** — the most popular metric has zero spread
2. **EBITDA/EV** — the book's "best single factor" is now dead
3. **P/B** — the Fama-French foundation is inverted
4. **Dividend Yield** — inverted at small/mid caps

## The Central Finding

**The value premium, as traditionally measured, has largely disappeared.** Of the 6 factors in the VC2 composite, 4 are broken or inverted. The composite underperforms its best single components because the dead factors dilute the signal.

The factors that survive — P/S, Buyback Yield, and Momentum — share one characteristic: they're based on the hardest-to-manipulate fundamentals (revenue, shares outstanding, price).

A modern "Value Composite" should use only P/S + Buyback Yield. Adding P/E, P/B, or EBITDA/EV makes it worse.
