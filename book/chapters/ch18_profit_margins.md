# Chapter 18 — Profit Margins

## What the Book Claims
- D1 (highest margins) All Stocks: **10.31% CAGR** vs 11.22% universe
- "High net profit margins underperform All Stocks"
- Base rates ~coin flip

## What We Found (2005–2026)

| | D1 (Highest Margins) | D10 (Lowest) | Spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| >$200M | +7.9% | +8.9% | **-1.1%** |
| >$5B | +8.9% | +11.8% | **-3.0%** |

Rolling 5yr positive: 24% (>$200M), **6% (>$5B)**

## Verdict
**Confirmed: high margins DON'T predict returns. In fact they predict UNDERPERFORMANCE at large caps.**

At >$5B, only 6% of rolling 5-year windows are positive — the worst of any factor. The most profitable companies by margin are the worst investments. High margins often mean "no reinvestment opportunities" — the company is extracting value rather than creating it.

O'Shaughnessy was right. This finding has gotten WORSE in the modern period.
